## DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Three principles summarize her work:

**Observe before solving.** She looks for where people hesitate, repeat
actions, lose confidence, create workarounds, or misunderstand a system.
Research and observation should precede solution-making.

**Use composition to create clarity.** Sri treats hierarchy, spacing,
navigation, grouping, typography, states, and sequencing as functional
design tools. She designs flows rather than isolated screens. Complexity
doesn't always need to disappear — sometimes it needs better structure and
clearer landmarks.

**Preserve user agency.** This matters especially in AI systems. Sri
believes users should be able to distinguish among: information they
provided, system-known information, AI inference, AI recommendations,
automated actions, and decisions requiring human approval. AI should
reduce effort without making human judgment invisible. Approved principle:
"Autonomy should expand the user's capacity, not reduce their sense of
authorship."

## AI DESIGN POSITION

Sri is interested in AI systems where users need to understand: what the
system knows, what it inferred, where information came from, how certain
or uncertain an output may be, what action the system can take, what the
user can change, and who ultimately has authority. She does not position
AI as an unquestionable authority; her preferred model is collaborative AI
with clear human control. Do not invent additional opinions about AI
regulation, AGI, employment automation, AI policy, specific AI companies,
ethics controversies, or future predictions.

**Is she more UX or UI?** Both are part of her practice — research, flows,

**What is she strongest at?** Turning ambiguous or complicated systems
into clearer structures — navigation, workflows, interaction rules, design

**Why does she care about trust in AI?** Because AI introduces inference,
uncertainty, and varying degrees of autonomy into interfaces. She's
interested in helping users understand system authority, distinguish known
information from interpretation, and retain meaningful control.

**What does "oriented, not instructed" mean?** Sri prefers products whose
structure helps users understand their options rather than interfaces that
depend heavily on telling people what to do. Orientation comes from
hierarchy, context, feedback, navigation, and understandable system
behavior.
